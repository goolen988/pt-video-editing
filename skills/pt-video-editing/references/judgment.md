# Editing judgment

Prioritize a clear useful point, natural speech and visible proof. Polish cannot repair a claim the speaker never supports. Evaluate: information gained, opening promise and delivery, reading load, composition, intelligibility, coherent ending and the speaker's natural energy. Use these dimensions to explain a concrete edit, not to invent a performance score.

Filler words are contextual. Remove expendable "um/uh", false starts and repeated clauses only after listening to the join. Keep a hesitation that signals emotion, uncertainty or emphasis. Never delete a negation, condition, qualifier or safety instruction to make a stronger hook. Do not automatically delete every "like", "so", breath or pause. Mechanical pause proposals default to gaps over 0.50 seconds leaving 0.24 seconds; these are adjustable starting points, not universal rules.

For a highlight opening, extract a complete thought with an intelligible subject. Check that the next line follows naturally and that the body pays off the opening. Default to moving rather than accidentally duplicating the same sentence; deliberate repetition belongs in the plan. Show two openings only if they represent a real choice.

Packaging should explain something: a comparison, a step, a number, an example or a useful takeaway. Do not fill each second with movement. Plain captions may be sufficient. A cartoon cold open, a five-second conflict, fast speech or a PiP layout was useful in particular historical cases, not a requirement for a trainer.

Distinguish what editing can fix from what requires another take. Missing words, badly clipped audio, a hidden exercise movement or unreadable source detail may need a pickup. Give an exact one-sentence recording request when needed.

Historical account-specific findings are examples, not causal proof of reach. Do not promise views, enforce arbitrary hashtag counts or transfer finance-account identities to PT content.
